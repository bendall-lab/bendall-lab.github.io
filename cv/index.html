Current Research Funding

  • 2025 - 2030
    Single cell dark matter in the context of HIV and substance use
    R01DA063076 (Nixon/Ndhlovu/Corley) · NIH/NIDA · $2,976,266 TC Y1-5 · 6.00 calendar months · Role: Co-Investigator
    • Submitted in response to RFA-DA-25-016, “Single Cell Opioid Responses in the Context of HIV (SCORCH) Program: Data Mining and Functional Validation (R01 Clinical Trial Not Allowed)”. The application aims to investigate the transcriptional and epigenetic regulatory states of transposable elements at the single cell level in the context of HIV, antiretroviral therapy, and cannabinoid use by applying data mining approaches in multi-omics datasets.
  • 2021 - 2026
    HOPE – HIV Obstruction by Programmed Epigenetics
    UM1AI164559 (Ott/Ndhlovu/Valente) · University of California San Francisco/NIH · $720,376.00 Subaward DC Y1-2 · 3.00 calendar months · Role: Subaward Co-Investigator
    • The HOPE mission is to find a cure for HIV using a novel “block-lock-and-excise” approach—which entails the long-term durable silencing of viral expression towards permanent excision of the latent provirus—leading to the control of the virus in the absence of therapy.
  • 2021 - 2026
    Regulatory Crosstalk Between Human Endogenous Retroviruses, HIV, and EBV, in Lymphoma
    R01CA260691 (Nixon/Cesarman) · NIH/NCI · $5,904,837.00 TC · 1.08 calendar months · Role: Co-Investigator
    • This R01 grant proposal will investigate how these activities intersect and synergize to promote the development of lymphoma in people living with HIV, and how translated HERV products could create neoantigenic targets for immune attack in diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL).

Past Research Funding

  • 2021 - 2024
    Elucidating the role of endogenous retrovirus expression in primary melanoma and metastasis
    61500657 (Bendall) · Weill Cornell Medicine (Fund for the Future) · $300,000.00 TC · 5.76 calendar months · Role: Principal Investigator
    • This proposal hypothesizes that locus-specific dysregulation of human endogenous retroviruses (HERV) expression is a significant predictor of metastatic risk in primary uveal melanoma (UM) and metastases associated HERVs modulate anti-tumor immunity and therefore may be harnessed to drive successful immune-based therapy in patients with UM. These hypotheses will be investigated by integrating multiomic data from primary and metastatic UM, leveraging the computational tools and databases that have been developed for analyzing transposable element expression.
  • 2022 - 2023
    The Role of Transposable Elements in Healthy Aging and in Alzheimer’s Disease
    R56AG07897001 (Nixon) · NIH/NIA · $865,823.00 TC · 1.92 calendar months · Role: Co-Investigator
    • This R01 proposal aims to investigate the role of human endogenous retroviruses as potent activators of innate immunity and their role in Alzheimer’s disease.
  • 2020 - 2022
    Novel approaches for relating genetic variation in endogenous retroviruses to function and disease
    R21HG011513 (Nixon/Powell) · NIH · $150,000.00 DC · 1.20 calendar months · Role: Co-Investigator
    • This R21 proposes to use a series of genetic and transcriptomic analyses to infer the etiological and functional role human endogenous retroviruses play in relation to heritable, polygenic disease states.
  • 2016 - 2022
    BELIEVE – Bench to Bed Enhanced Lymphocyte Infusions to Engineer Viral Eradication
    UM1AI126617 (Nixon) · NIH/NIAID · Role: Co-Investigator
    • This proposal hypothesizes that enhancing patients’ autologous lymphocytes ex vivo and re-infusing them, along with latency reversing agents, and specific targeting to sites where latent virus resides, could lead to eradication of latently infected cells and potentially a cure for HIV-infected patients.
  • 2016 - 2019
    HIV induced anti-cancer HERV immunity in prostate, breast and colon cancers
    R01CA206488 (Nixon) · NIH/NCI · Role: Graduate Research Assistant
    • This proposal hypothesizes that HIV reactivation of HERV’s stimulates anti-HERV immunity, which specifically recognizes HERVs also expressed in prostate, colon and breast cancers; and that these HIV induced HERV specific immune responses target HERVs that are expressed in breast, colon or prostate cancer.
