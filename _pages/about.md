I am an Associate Research Professor in the Department of Biostatistics and Bioinformatics at the Milken Institute School of Public Health, The George Washington University. My lab develops computational methods to measure and interpret the expression of transposable elements (TEs) and human endogenous retroviruses (HERVs), the "dark matter" of the transcriptome, and applies them to questions in cancer, HIV, autoimmunity, aging, and psychiatric disease.

Our open-source software includes [Telescope](https://github.com/mlbendall/telescope) for locus-specific TE quantification from RNA-seq, Stellarscope for TE expression in single-cell data, and HAPHPIPE for viral haplotype reconstruction. I also contributed to Pathoscope and Bioconda.

Before joining GWU in 2025, I was an Assistant Professor of Computational Biology Research in Medicine at Weill Cornell Medicine (2019–2025). I earned my Ph.D. in Genomics and Bioinformatics from GWU, and my M.S. in Biology and B.S. in Bioinformatics from Brigham Young University.
